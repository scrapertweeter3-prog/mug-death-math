A chipped rim is a purchase order

A four-person kitchen keeps twelve mugs and loses three a year to rim chips. At nine dollars a mug the replacement bill runs twenty-seven dollars every year, and it never closes out. A twenty-six dollar mug on a twelve-year clock costs 2.17 dollars annually, one purchase covering the whole stretch.

Annualized, the nine-dollar mug dies at 4.50 a year on a two-year chip clock. Across those same twelve years it is six buys and 54 dollars stacked against a single 26-dollar buy. The crossover sits at 2.9 years: the cheap mug has to reach its third birthday just to tie on annual cost, and most cabinet deaths happen well before that.

Rim chip, handle crack, glaze wear each run on their own clock, so a ranking by death mode tells you more than a price tag ever will.
